# Budgeting Basics

## The 50/30/20 Rule

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting framework for splitting after-tax
income into three buckets: 50% for needs (rent, groceries, utilities, minimum
debt payments), 30% for wants (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions), and
20% for savings and debt repayment beyond the minimum. It is a starting
guideline rather than a strict rule, and the exact split should be adjusted
based on cost of living, income level, and financial goals.

## Emergency Funds

An emergency fund is money set aside specifically to cover unexpected
expenses or a loss of income, such as medical bills, urgent home or vehicle
repairs, or job loss. A common guideline is to keep 3 to 6 months of
essential living expenses in an easily accessible, low-risk account (such as
a savings account or liquid fund), separate from everyday spending money and
long-term investments. The right size of an emergency fund depends on job
stability, number of dependents, and existing insurance coverage.

## Cash Flow and Net Worth

Cash flow is the difference between money coming in (income) and money going
out (expenses) over a period of time. Positive cash flow means more is
earned than spent. Net worth is the value of everything owned (assets) minus
everything owed (liabilities) at a point in time. Tracking both over time is
a more complete picture of financial health than looking at either alone.
