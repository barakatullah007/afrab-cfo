# Investing Basics

## Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs)

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed amount
of money into a mutual fund at regular intervals, typically monthly,
regardless of the fund's price at that time. This approach uses rupee-cost
averaging: more fund units are purchased when prices are low and fewer when
prices are high, which smooths out the average purchase cost over time. SIPs
are commonly used for long-term goals because they encourage disciplined,
automatic investing rather than trying to time the market.

## Index Funds

An index fund is a type of mutual fund or exchange-traded fund (ETF) built
to track the performance of a specific market index, such as the Nifty 50 or
S&P 500, by holding the same securities in the same proportions as that
index. Because they are passively managed rather than actively picking
stocks, index funds typically have lower expense ratios than actively
managed funds. They are widely used as a low-cost way to get diversified
market exposure.

## Diversification

Diversification means spreading investments across different asset classes,
sectors, or geographies so that poor performance in one area does not
disproportionately affect the overall portfolio. It does not eliminate risk,
but it reduces the impact of any single investment performing badly.

## Compound Interest

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and
the interest that has already been added to it. Because earnings themselves
start earning further returns, growth accelerates over time — the earlier
and longer money is invested, the larger the effect of compounding.
